The first job after graduation was short, and also good. Friends around me kept saying that staying in one place for three years was already a long time. Looking back, those three years also feel as short as a single moment — much like Kahneman describes in *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, where thinking comes in two systems: fast and slow.

While I was inside those days, everything ran on "fast thinking": pushed along by the commute, the todo list and the OKRs, moving forward like a wheel with no time to look closely. Only after leaving did "slow thinking" kick in, taking those fragments I had once dismissed one by one apart, and recognising them again.

So those three years are both "a long time", as my friends said, and as short as the blink of an eye. What was fast was the experience; what was slow is the understanding. What was short was the time; what is long is the echo it left behind.
